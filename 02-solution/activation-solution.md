## Aha moment

_The exact moment FinWise becomes "my money, finally clear."_

A new trial user links their business bank account and, within seconds, sees one
personal answer they didn't have before: how many months of cash they have left,
where their money went, or what they'll owe in tax. They get it without typing a
single number. They then set a low-cash alert, so FinWise keeps watching their
money for them.

The feature is a dashboard. The realization is: "I was guessing about my money.
Now I know, and I didn't have to do anything to find out."

**Behavior signal:** in the first session, the user (1) links a bank account,
(2) confirms one auto-sorted category, and (3) sets a low-cash alert.

The Aha happens at the top of screen 4, in the big number card. The choice on
screen 2 decides which number appears there.

## Why this activates

_The activation logic: what changes, and why it converts trial users._

**What changes:** the user sees a real, personal number about their own business
in the first session, before FinWise asks them to set anything up.

**The friction I removed:** the company profile form (business name, tax ID,
industry, fiscal year) and chart-of-accounts setup. Screen 4 needs none of this.
FinWise asks for it later, only when a feature needs it. My hypothesis: every
field before value is a chance to quit, and small-business owners in a trial
have little patience for setup work that shows them nothing.

**Why it predicts retention:**
- **Linking a bank** means data keeps flowing in. FinWise gets more useful each
  day with no extra work, and leaving means losing that history.
- **Confirming a category** shows the user trusts the numbers enough to correct
  them.
- **Setting an alert** turns a one-time answer into an ongoing job. FinWise now
  watches cash for the user, and each alert brings them back.

**Why it converts (reverse trial):** the lead number and the alert come from a
paid feature (the cash forecast), tagged "Premium · free in trial." On day 15,
the user loses a warning system they chose to switch on, not a feature they
never touched. That loss is the reason to pay.

**Supporting choices:**
- A one-question quiz (screen 2) tailors the first answer to the user's own goal.
- "Try with sample data" keeps a path to value for users not ready to link a bank.
- Trust lines on screen 3 (read-only, login stays with the bank, encryption)
  target the step with the highest drop-off risk.

**How to test it:** compare 90-day retention and trial-to-paid conversion for
users who complete all three signal actions in session 1 against those who
don't. Linking may only mark users who were already committed, so also A/B test
the trust lines on screen 3. If a higher link rate also raises retention, linking
does real work. If retention stays flat, linking only marked intent, and the
signal needs to change.
